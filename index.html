What we stand for

A wood, a fire, and a reason to keep it small.

Sticks and Worms began as forest school sessions run by one teacher who would always rather be outside. It recently became Sticks and Worms Nature Network CIC — a community interest company — so that what it earns goes back into running more sessions for more children, rather than into anyone's pocket.

EST.
CIC Newly established Community Interest Company.
Not-for-profit · run for the children who come.

  • Outdoors, whatever the sky's doing

    Rain is half of it. We go out in the weather and dress for it — that's where the good days happen.

  • Child-led, not clipboard-led

    Forest school follows the children. They choose what to get stuck into, and the learning comes with it.

  • Room for every kind of child

    Lots of experience with additional needs, and a separate SEND nature club too. Tell us what your child needs.

  • Mixed ages, together

    Four to fourteen in one group. Older ones look out for younger ones — the way it works in the real world.

What the children actually do

Forest school is learner-led, so no two days run the same. These are the things that come round most often.

The fire circle

Children help build and light the fire, and learn how to do it safely themselves.

Cooking on the fire

Campfire food made outdoors — do tell us about any allergies beforehand.

Dens & hammocks

Real shelters built from what's around, knots and all.

Bushcraft & skills

Tool use, knots and the practical things that come with being outdoors.

Woodland games

Traditional outdoor games — the loud, running-about kind.

Seasonal crafts

Making and art from whatever the season gives us.

Nature trails

Getting out around the site and finding what's living there.

Hands in the soil

Digging, planting and growing — the muddy end of the day.

Who runs it

Hi, I'm Sahar.

I'm a primary school teacher and I work right across the year groups. Outside the classroom I'm a qualified Forest School Leader and a Scout leader for 10 to 14 year olds — so a mixed-age group is completely normal to me, not a problem to solve.

I've spent over eight years working with children, including a lot of time supporting children with additional needs. I'm a qualified Lowland Leader too, so I lead walks for all ages, and honestly I'll take any excuse to be outside — walking, camping, or knee-deep in a wood with a group of children.

The practical bits, because they matter: I'm outdoor first aid trained, and I'm on the DBS update service with a full enhanced check.

Before you come

The practical bits

Everything worth knowing the night before, so nobody turns up in the wrong shoes.

Wrap up warm

Old clothes you don't mind ruining, wellies or boots that fasten on, waterproofs and layers. It's a canal bank — colder than town, and we're out in it all day.

Bring a packed lunch

We break for lunch, so send food from home. Drinks, snacks and all the equipment are provided.

No nuts on site

Please check what you pack — nothing containing nuts. And tell us about any allergies beforehand, since the children do cooking.

Toilets & getting there

The site has compost toilets. It's Canalside Activity and Environment Centre, Bonsall Street, Blackburn, BB2 4DD.

Booking & questions

How booking works

Sessions are booked through Eequ. Pick your dates on the listing and you'll get confirmation details. Places are limited, so it's best to book early.

You can cancel up to 48 hours before a session for a full refund.

Because each session is a leisure activity on a set date, the 14-day cooling-off period under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 doesn't apply. As with any activity, it's the booker's responsibility to be satisfied with the safety arrangements before booking.
