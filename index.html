What we stand for

A wood, a fire, and a reason to keep it going.

Sticks and Worms began as forest school sessions run by one teacher who would always rather be outside. Earlier this year it became Sticks and Worms Nature Network CIC — a community interest company — so what it earns goes back into running more sessions for more children, rather than into anyone's pocket.

  • Outdoors, whatever the sky's doing

In a session

What the children actually do

Forest school is learner-led, so no two days run the same. These are the things that come round most often.

The fire circle

Children help build and light the fire, and learn how to do it safely themselves.

Cooking on the fire

Campfire food made outdoors — do tell us about any allergies beforehand.

Dens & hammocks

Real shelters built from what's around, knots and all.

Bushcraft & skills

Tool use, knots and the practical things that come with being outdoors.

Woodland games

Traditional outdoor games — the loud, running-about kind.

Seasonal crafts

Making and art from whatever the season gives us.

Nature trails

Getting out around the site and finding what's living there.

Hands in the soil

Digging, planting and growing — the muddy end of the day.

Sahar · Forest School Leader
Who runs it

Hi, I'm Sahar.

I'm a qualified Forest School Leader, and have been since 2018. I began delivering sessions within the primary school where I was teaching. After a decade of teaching in a primary setting, I've recently moved to focus on Forest School experiences, particularly for home-educated children across the North West.

Alongside Forest School, I also lead walks for groups of all backgrounds and abilities, promoting connection with nature and wellbeing through accessible outdoor experiences.

Earlier this year, I founded Sticks and Worms as a Community Interest Company (CIC) to expand my work within the green sector and deepen my commitment to outdoor education and nature-based engagement.

The practical bits, because they matter: I'm outdoor first aid trained, and on the DBS update service with a full enhanced check.

What I care about

  • Inspiring children to connect with and care for the conservation of land and wildlife.
  • Fostering a love and respect for animals through education and meaningful interaction with all living things.
  • Advocating for greater accessibility and inclusion in our National Parks and green spaces.
  • Promoting the joy and wellbeing that come from hiking and spending time outdoors.

My favourite place? Buttermere Lake.

Before you come

The practical bits

Everything worth knowing the night before, so nobody turns up in the wrong shoes.

Wrap up warm

Old clothes you don't mind ruining, wellies or boots that fasten on, waterproofs and layers. Outdoors all day means colder than town.

Bring a packed lunch

We break for lunch, so send food from home. Drinks, snacks and all the equipment are provided.

No nuts, please

Please check what you pack — nothing containing nuts. And tell us about any allergies beforehand, since the children do cooking.

Where we meet

The Blackburn home ed group runs at Canalside, Bonsall Street, BB2 4DD (compost toilets on site). The summer club is at Ashworth Valley, Rochdale.

Booking & questions

How booking works

Home ed sessions are booked through Eequ — pick your dates on the listing and you'll get confirmation details. Places are limited, so it's best to book early. For the summer holiday club, email or call us.

You can cancel up to 48 hours before a session for a full refund.

Because each session is a leisure activity on a set date, the 14-day cooling-off period under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 doesn't apply. As with any activity, it's the booker's responsibility to be satisfied with the safety arrangements before booking.

It depends on the session — some are drop-off and some are stay-and-play. The details are on the Eequ listing when you book, or message us and we'll tell you straight away.

Yes. Mixed ages are the norm here, not the exception. Activities are open-ended enough that a five year old and a twelve year old can do the same thing at their own level.

Very welcome. There's a lot of experience here supporting children with additional needs, and Sticks and Worms also runs a dedicated SEND nature club. Message before you book and tell us what your child needs — it's much easier to plan for than to react to.

Children work with real fire and real tools, because that's what forest school is — but always with a brief first, clear rules, close supervision and a risk assessment behind it. The leader is outdoor first aid trained with a first aid kit on site.

We go out anyway — rain is half of forest school. Send waterproofs and a full change of clothes. We'd only cancel for genuinely unsafe conditions, and we'd let you know as early as we could.
